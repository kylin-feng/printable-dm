What Goes on a DM Screen

A DM screen is prime real estate: four panels facing you that should answer questions in under two seconds. Here is how to choose what earns that space.

The rule: frequency × urgency

Every square inch of screen should host information you look up often and mid-action. The condition ‘Frightened’ (every session, mid-attack) earns a panel; the armor table (once per level-up, between sessions) does not. Audit your screen with that one rule and half your clutter disappears.

The core four, facing you

1. Conditions, one line each

Conditions are the most looked-up rule in 5e and the cheapest to summarize. One line of effect, one line of what ends it. Our cheat sheet inserts put all fifteen on a single panel — or use condition cards if you prefer handing the rule to the player.

2. Common DCs and the difficulty ladder

Very Easy 5 / Easy 10 / Medium 15 / Hard 20 / Very Hard 25 / Nearly Impossible 30 — plus two or three concrete examples per ability so your brain has a hook under pressure (“Hard Wisdom: resist a banshee's wail”).

3. Death saves, dying, stabilization

Death save DC 10, three successes stabilize, three failures die, damage while at 0 HP = instant failure (crit = two), 1 HP on a natural 20. You will run this rule at the worst possible moment; it belongs on paper.

4. Cover, opportunity attacks, and grappling

+2 / +5 AC & Dex saves for half and three-quarters cover, total cover blocks targeting; opportunity attacks trigger on leaving reach not the 5-foot square; grapple and shove are Athletics vs Athletics/Acrobatics contests, not saves.

The outer panels, facing the players

Player-facing space is for shared state: the current initiative order clipped where everyone can see it, condition cards folded over the top edge, and — the classic — a name-generation table or common-word glossary for your setting. The outer panels reduce the questions aimed at you; that is their whole job.

What to take OFF your screen

  • Price lists and trade goods. Shopping happens between sessions; look it up then.
  • The full armor table. Once armor is bought, it is on the character sheet.
  • Monster stat blocks you taped up last month. Screens accrete. Prune monthly.
  • Anything you have genuinely memorized. Free the space for something you have not.
Session state belongs on the screen too. A small sticky note with tonight's NPC names and the three secrets you intend to reveal turns a bland session into a tight one. Our session notes template pairs well here: notes inside, rules on the panels.

Screens vs. laptops vs. no screen at all

A laptop holds infinite rules but also your dice roller, your music, and your attention. A physical screen answers conditions in two seconds and never pings. No screen at all — the open-table style — maximizes presence and trust, at the cost of memorizing more or prepping index cards. All three styles work; choose deliberately, and stock whichever surface you use with the same core four.

Quick checklist

  • All 15 conditions, one line each
  • DC ladder with examples
  • Dying rules and death saves
  • Cover, opportunity attacks, grapple/shove
  • Initiative order visible to players
  • Tonight's names + secrets on a sticky note
