Metal vs Resin Dice: Which Should You Buy?

The honest comparison every dice buyer deserves — weight, feel, durability, price and table safety — plus which set to buy first when you can only buy one.

Every tabletop player hits this question within a year of the hobby, and the internet's answer is usually “buy both” — which is technically correct and completely unhelpful. Here is the actual comparison, number by number.

The honest numbers

Metal diceResin dice
Weight (7-piece set)120–160 g25–45 g
Typical price$20–$60$8–$25
Drop testSurvives; dents soft tablesChips if rolled off tables repeatedly
BalanceSolid castings roll trueGood sets roll true; cheap sets can bias

What metal actually feels like

A zinc-alloy d20 weighs about what an entire resin set does. That weight is the entire product: the thunk when it lands, the authority when it rolls, the small ritual of shaking a fistful of cold metal before a save that matters. The downsides are equally real — metal dice will mark a bare wooden table (a folding tray is not optional, it is part of the purchase), they are loud in shared spaces, and a full metal set costs two to three times its resin twin.

What resin does better

Modern resin sets come with sharp edges, deep layered colors and glitter pours that metal cannot imitate at any price — the visual variety is unmatched. They're quiet, light, table-friendly, and cheap enough to own one per character (which is, let's be honest, how this works). Two honest caveats: very cheap resin sets can arrive with visible mold seams or off-center numbers, and sharp-edged sets chip if you roll them off the table every session. Buy from shops that hand-inspect sets before shipping — it matters more than the material does.

The balance question, answered

Players ask whether metal dice roll “fairer.” Both materials roll true when manufactured properly; neither material is inherently more random. What actually biases dice is air bubbles inside resin (visible in cheap sets) and worn plating on very old metal dice. If you can see through a die and spot a bubble near the core, that die belongs in the villain jar.

So which do you buy first?

If you play mostly at home: start metal. The tray comes with the lifestyle, and the weight genuinely changes how rolling feels. Our friends at Dice Goblin bundle a metal set with a folding tray and pouch for about the price most shops charge for the dice alone.

If you play at stores, cons or other people's tables: start resin — quiet, light, forgiving — and upgrade to metal once you know your table manners won't get you disinvited.

If you're buying a gift: metal, always. Nobody unwraps zinc alloy and stays calm.

Care, briefly

  • Metal: dry storage, occasional soft-cloth wipe, never leave a set in a damp bag (plating dislikes long baths).
  • Resin: keep them off direct sunlight for months at a time (yellowing), and away from the edge of the table.
  • Both: roll in a tray. A snapping folding tray costs less than replacing one d20 you loved.

The real answer

The internet was right: eventually you own both — resin for travel and tavern nights, metal for the boss fight. But the order matters less than buying a set someone inspected before it shipped. Roll well.
