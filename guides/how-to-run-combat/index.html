How to Run Combat in D&D 5e

The six-step combat loop, the rules you actually look up mid-fight, and the three habits that separate a smooth fight from a slog — a cheat sheet for new DMs.

The combat loop in six steps

  1. Establish positions. Where is everyone when steel gets drawn? Set the scene before any rolls — it prevents the “wait, I was behind the pillar” argument.
  2. Check for surprise. Compare the ambushers' group Stealth against the defenders' passive Perception. Surprised creatures skip their actions and reactions on their first turn only — surprise is not a free round.
  3. Roll initiative. Everyone rolls d20 + Dexterity. Identical monsters share one roll and one turn — the single biggest speed-up in the rules. Ties go to higher Dexterity. Write the whole order down before anyone acts; our initiative tracker sheet exists for exactly this.
  4. Take turns, top to bottom. On a turn: move up to speed, take one Action, take one Bonus Action only if a feature grants it, and interact with one object for free. Reactions happen any time they're triggered.
  5. Wrap each round. When the order returns to the top, a round has passed — tick it off. Durations (1 minute = 10 rounds) count down from there.
  6. End the fight honestly. Most monsters flee at half strength or surrender when the tide turns. A fight that ends on morale is a fight that ends interesting.

The rules you look up mid-fight (so learn these first)

Attack rolls and saving throws

Attack: d20 + ability modifier + proficiency vs the target's AC. A natural 20 always hits and doubles the dice; a natural 1 always misses. Saves: the caster's DC is 8 + proficiency + casting ability; the target rolls d20 + the named save, meeting or beating the DC succeeds.

Death saves

At 0 HP a creature falls unconscious and rolls a DC 10 death save at the start of each of its turns: three successes stabilize, three failures die. Any damage at 0 HP is an automatic failure (a critical hit is two). A natural 20 brings the creature back to 1 HP. This rule fires at the worst moment — keep it on paper, not in memory, with our DM screen cheat sheet.

Cover

Half cover (+2 AC and Dex saves): another creature, furniture, a tree trunk. Three-quarters (+5): an arrow slit, thick fog. Total cover: can't be targeted at all.

Opportunity attacks

Triggered when a hostile leaves your reach — not when it circles around inside it, not on teleportation, not when it's pushed. Disengage prevents it. One reaction per round, so a creature can only punish one exit.

Grappling and shoving

Athletics check contested by the target's Athletics or Acrobatics (their choice). Win the grapple: their speed is 0. Win the shove: knocked prone or pushed 5 feet. No saving throws involved — it's a contest.

Three habits that keep fights fast

1. Call the on-deck player. “Aria, you're up — Thorin, you're on deck.” Half of combat slowness is players who didn't know their turn was coming. The on-deck call eliminates it.

2. Track state on paper, not in your head. HP as tally marks, concentration as a checkbox, bloodied as a circled name. Round four of a seven-creature fight is where mental tracking collapses — a printed combat tracker with HP pips makes round ten as calm as round one.

3. Average damage for hordes. When six goblins hit, don't roll six dice — take the average (it's printed on the stat block) or roll once and apply it to all. Nobody notices; everybody notices the wait you removed.

Common new-DM mistakes

  • Rolling initiative per monster. Roll per monster type. The wolf pack shares one initiative.
  • Forgetting lair actions. They trigger at initiative count 20, losing ties — write “LAIR: 20” into the roster like a combatant.
  • Stopping the fight to check a rule. Rule call in ten seconds, write it down, look it up after the session. Momentum is worth more than a perfect ruling.
  • One-note monsters. Give each monster one tactic: the wolf pack trips, the archer kite, the ogre shoves into hazards. Fights get memorable and you never open the stat block twice.
