What's inside

  • Eight combatant blocks — name, initiative, AC, and a big HP pip field: shade pips down as damage lands, no mental subtraction mid-round.
  • Concentration & marks row — tick-boxes for concentration, bless, poisoned and ‘bloodied at half’ per combatant.
  • Turn wheel & notes strip — round counter plus a wide strip for lair actions, legendary action budget and environmental effects.

How to use it

Use one block per combatant — monsters and players both — and pre-fill the HP pips to each creature’s maximum. When damage lands, cross off pips from the right; when healing lands, redraw them. The pip system is slower to start than writing numbers but radically faster in rounds three through ten, which is where combats actually live.

The marks row is for the three states DMs forget most: concentration (roll that Con save when the wizard takes a hit), conditions with saves, and bloodied — which most tables use as a cue for phase changes and morale checks.

Printing tip: One sheet covers eight combatants; print page 2 double-sided so the notes strip is always on the back of a full fight.

FAQ

How many combatants fit on one sheet?

Eight blocks per sheet; print two sheets for big set-piece battles.

What are the HP pips for?

Shade one pip per hit point (or per 5 HP for big monsters). It removes mid-round arithmetic entirely.

Does it replace the initiative tracker?

It complements it: initiative tracker for order, combat tracker for state. Small fights need only the tracker.
